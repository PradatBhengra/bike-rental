# Repair and troubleshooting

Small problems can often be fixed on the spot. If you are not sure, stop and call the helpline on 0651-000-0000 (06:00 to 22:00). Never ride a bike whose brakes do not work.

## 1 Flat tyre

### 1.1 What to do

Do not keep riding on a flat tyre: it damages the wheel. Walk the bike to the nearest station; any station can swap the bike or fix the tyre, even if you did not start there. Punctures from normal riding cost nothing.

### 1.2 Slow leak

If the tyre is soft but not flat, any station will pump it for free. Every station has a pump at the desk.

## 2 Chain came off

### 2.1 Putting the chain back

Shift to the smallest front ring. Lift the rear wheel, push the rear derailleur arm forward to slacken the chain, and lay the chain over the bottom of the front ring. Turn the pedals slowly backwards and the chain will climb back on. The chain is oily: wipe your hands on the cloth in the bike bag.

### 2.2 Chain keeps coming off

Stop riding and call the helpline. A chain that keeps dropping usually means a bent derailleur, which staff need to fix.

## 3 Brakes

### 3.1 Squealing brakes

Squealing in rain or dust is normal and goes away as the brakes dry. It is not a fault.

### 3.2 Weak brakes

If a lever pulls all the way to the handlebar, or the bike does not stop well, stop riding at once. Lock the bike somewhere safe and call the helpline, or walk it to the nearest station.

## 4 Breakdown during a ride

### 4.1 If the bike cannot be ridden

Call the helpline with your booking code and location. Staff tell you the nearest station to walk to, or collect the bike within the city. Time spent waiting for staff after a breakdown is not charged as late.

### 4.2 E-bike problems

For e-bike display codes, see the e-bike error codes guide. A Volt with an empty battery or a motor fault still works as a normal, heavier bike.
